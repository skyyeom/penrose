# Interview Guide

> Part of the [player research kit](README.md) (#28). **Length:** 30–40 min. **Rule:** ask about past behavior, not hypotheticals, and don't mention Penrose until Section 6.

---

## 0. Before the interview (2 min)

- Participant ID, date, persona group (P1 / P2 / P3)
- Recording? Only with consent

## 1. Introduction and consent (3 min)

> "Thanks for joining. I'm researching how people find and play story-driven games. There are no right or wrong answers, and I'm not selling anything. You can skip any question or stop at any time. Is it OK if I take notes [and record]?"

## 2. Games they play (10 min) → A1, A3, A4, A7

1. Tell me about the last story-driven game you **finished**. What was it?
2. How did you first hear about it? *(probe: a video, a friend, the store page, TikTok?)*
3. What made you actually buy or download it? Was there a moment you decided?
4. Did you try a free version or demo first? *(if yes: what made you go from the free version to buying?)*
5. How much did it cost, and did that feel fair for how long it was?
6. After finishing, did you go back to it? *(probe: other endings, wikis, videos, theories)*
7. Tell me about a game you **stopped playing**. What happened?
8. *(P2)* Tell me about a game with dress-up or character customization you played recently. What did you like about it? Was there anything that felt condescending?

## 3. Horror and boundaries (7 min) → A5, A8

9. When a horror game gets graphic, what do you usually do? *(probe: keep going, look away, change settings, stop)*
10. Have you ever used a content warning or intensity setting? Tell me about that time.
11. *(optional, gentle)* Some games deal with pressure about how people look. Has a game ever done that in a way that felt true to you? Or one that got it wrong?
    - Don't ask about the participant's own body, history or numbers. If they share something personal, listen, thank them, and don't dig.

## 4. Culture (4 min) → A6

12. Tell me about a game or show set in a culture different from yours that you enjoyed. What made it work, or not?
13. When something wasn't explained, did that make it more interesting or more confusing?

## 5. The quiz test (5 min) → A2

Show three quiz questions **as cards**, with no explanation:
- *If you were a houseplant, where would you want to be placed?* (windowsill · coffee table · office lobby · café)
- *Which sound would you most like to wake up to?* (someone cooking breakfast · a voice message · your alarm · phone notifications)
- *If you were a painting, where would you want to hang?* (hallway · above the bed · boardroom · gallery)

14. Answer these for fun.
15. What do you think a quiz like this is trying to find out about you?
    - Write down their exact guess. Don't correct it.

## 6. Concept reaction (5 min), only now

> "Here's the game I'm making: a hand-drawn psychological horror game that looks like a harmless self-improvement game. Every 'improvement' you choose is actually hurting the character, and your choices decide how it ends."

16. What's your first reaction?
17. Who would you send this to? *(stronger signal than "would you buy it?")*
18. What would make you **not** play it?
19. Where would you expect to hear about a game like this?

## 7. Wrap-up (2 min)

20. Is there anything I should have asked but didn't?
21. Can I contact you for a playtest later? *(optional)*

> "Thank you. Since we touched on some sensitive topics, here are some support resources in case they're ever useful."

Share the support resources.

---

## Creator interview (short, 15–20 min) → A9

For C1 (Let's Players and streamers):
1. How do you decide which games to play on your channel?
2. Tell me about a video that got demonetized or age-restricted. What caused it?
3. Has music ever caused a copyright claim on your videos?
4. What do you look for in the first 10 minutes of a horror game?
5. How do you get keys? What makes you open a developer's email?
6. *(concept)* Would a game about appearance pressure be risky for your channel? What would make it safe?
