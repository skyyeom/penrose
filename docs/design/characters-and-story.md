**Why a blank protagonist works here:** the less the protagonist has a fixed personality, the more the player's choices *are* the protagonist's choices. That's the "You did this" pillar.

## 2. The Gumiho (concept open question 2)

The gumiho is the Korean nine-tailed fox who eats human livers or hearts to become human **[Decided]** as folklore inspiration. Many retellings add a twist: the fox can become human **only by going 1,000 days without eating**. The way to become human is to *stop* consuming, not to consume more.

That twist is Penrose's message exactly: the protagonist consumes pieces of themselves to become "acceptable", and the only way to become whole is to stop.

| Option | How it would work | Fit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Guide | The fox leads the player toward the way out | Makes the game too easy to read |
| Villain | The fox tempts the player to accept upgrades | Undercuts "You did this": blames a monster |
| The protagonist | The protagonist is the gumiho | Strong, but literal; competes with the Reflection |
| **Symbol of the true self** ✅ | A fox appears in every loop, just out of reach, and runs when approached | Best fit |

**Recommendation: symbol of the true self. [Proposed]**
- **Loop 1:** a fox glimpsed at the edge of a room, gone when the camera pans.
- **Loop 2:** the fox watches from the stairs; it runs when approached **[Decided]** concept idea.
- **Loop 3:** the fox is thin, hungry, eating something off-screen (each upgrade "feeds" it).
- **Loop 4+:** the fox has lost tails.
- **Good / Special:** the fox stops running. In the Special ending it sits beside the protagonist and has all nine tails.
- **Resistance counts as "days without eating"** ([systems §6](systems.md)): the folklore is built into the mechanic, for players who know it.

**Gumiho-inspired outfits [Decided]:** black, pale, illusion-based, alluring and otherworldly. Available as Social Media Room "looks" that become harder to take off.

## 3. How autobiographical? (concept open question 5)

**Recommendation: fiction rooted in truth. [Proposed]**

- The protagonist, family and events are **fictional**, so no real people are depicted and players can see themselves in the story.
- The **feelings and pressures are real**, drawn from the creator's lived experience. That's the game's main selling point ([market research](../01-market-research.md)).
- An optional **creator's note** after the first ending says the game comes from lived experience, without naming anyone.

## 5. Story by act

| Act | Loop | Beat |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | — | A harmless personality quiz. Character creation |
| 1 · The Makeover | 1 | A happy first week after graduation. Everyone is proud and helpful. Every room has a small, kind suggestion. The first Bathroom visit asks, "How do you like what you see?" (0–10). The day ends with sleep |
| 2 · Cracks | 2 | "The next day" is the same day. Small repetitions; NPCs deny repeating themselves. The Reflection first speaks: "What are you afraid they'll find out?" (core belief). The rose has fewer petals. The fox watches from the stairs |
| 3 · The Reveal | 3 | The corridor leads back to the start without a cut. Every mini-game shows what it really was; the mirror shows every upgrade. NPCs praise, word for word, while looking at the damage. The Reflection asks, "Why do you keep coming back?" The endings become reachable |
| 4 · Breakdown | 4+ | The door of white light leads back to the start. Rooms bleed into each other; text corrupts; "New room discovered!" for the fifth time. The game still never admits it's looping |
| Ending | — | Bad, Neutral, Good or Special ([endings](endings.md)), then the debrief |

**The plot twist [Decided]:** "We guide the player toward perfection, and the twist is that we're breaking her (or him)." The player realizes they have been the one doing the damage.
