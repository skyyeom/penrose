> **What this doc is:** detailed profiles of the people Penrose is for. Persona templates usually describe roles inside B2B client companies (a Procurement Manager, a Sales Operations Analyst). ## 2. Player personas

### P1 · Alex Rivera, the Lore Diver (primary)

> *"I want a game that's still in my head a week later, and I want my choices to actually change something."*

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Age / location** | 22, Chicago, USA |
| **Life** | Final-year university student (psychology minor); part-time job at a campus café; lifts weights three times a week and jokes about it, but quietly compares himself to gym influencers |
| **Gaming habits** | 8–12 h a week on PC and Steam Deck. Finishes short narrative games in one or two sittings, then replays for other endings and reads the wiki |
| **Favorite games** | DDLC, OMORI, Slay the Princess, Inscryption, Mouthwashing |
| **How he finds games** | Video essays (Jacob Geller, analysis channels), Reddit, "games like DDLC" lists, Steam's *More Like This* |
| **Buys when** | A trusted creator covers it; "Overwhelmingly Positive" reviews; under $20; under 5 hours |
| **Goals** | Be surprised; decode symbols and hidden systems; find every ending; talk about it online |
| **Frustrations** | Choices that change nothing; twists spoiled by trailers; padding; "deep" games that are only edgy |
| **Penrose scenario** | Plays blind on a Friday night. Notices the repeating wallpaper in Loop 2 and starts doubting the game. The Loop 3 reveal hits hard because he *played* the training mini-game. Reaches the Neutral ending, reads the debrief, and is shocked the houseplant question meant something. Replays for the Special ending and posts his theory on Reddit |
| **What Penrose must do for him** | Choices that visibly change endings; hidden systems revealed at the end; layered symbolism (rose, gumiho, Penrose stairs); a male protagonist option with the Stronger path |

### P2 · Sofia Kim, the Dark-Aesthetic Dress-Up Fan (primary)

> *"I love cute things that turn out to be creepy. But I hate when a game makes fun of girls for caring how they look."*

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Age / location** | 19, Toronto, Canada (Korean-Canadian) |
| **Life** | First-year design student; follows webtoons and K-beauty creators; has a skincare routine she half-loves, half-resents |
| **Gaming habits** | Laptop and phone; plays itch.io browser games between classes; buys a few cheap Steam games a year, usually on sale |
| **Favorite games** | Needy Streamer Overload, KinitoPET, Money for Girls, itch.io horror dress-up games |
| **How she finds games** | TikTok edits, Tumblr, Pinterest boards, itch.io front page, friends' Discord |
| **Buys when** | The art style stops her scrolling; there's a free demo or itch.io version first; $5–15 |
| **Goals** | A distinctive aesthetic; character customization; a "wait, WHAT" moment she can clip or screenshot |
| **Frustrations** | Shallow cute-horror that's all jump scares; games that sneer at fashion or beauty interests; being ambushed by graphic content without warning |
| **Penrose scenario** | Finds a 15-second TikTok of the skincare mini-game turning wrong. Plays the free itch.io slice in her browser, wishlists on Steam. Buys it at launch, plays on Softened intensity, and makes fan art of the gumiho outfits |
| **What Penrose must do for her** | A striking black/white/red style; meaningful customization; a free itch.io slice; content warnings and intensity settings; never mocking the player's interest in beauty, only the system around it |

### P3 · Hana Lee, the Meaning Seeker (secondary)

> *"I don't need a game to be scary. I need it to be true."*

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Age / location** | 28, Seoul, South Korea (also represents Korean-diaspora players abroad) |
| **Life** | Marketing coordinator; had double-eyelid surgery at 19 as a graduation gift; still hears relatives' comments at every holiday |
| **Gaming habits** | 3–5 h a week; story games on PC and Switch; reads reviews before buying; finishes what she buys |
| **Favorite games** | Consume Me, Mouthwashing, 1000xRESIST, GRIS, Fran Bow |
| **How she finds games** | Steam reviews, Bluesky, game award lists, articles about "games about mental health" |
| **Buys when** | Reviews say it handles its subject with care; it's made by someone with lived experience |
| **Goals** | To feel understood; to find words for something she lived through; to finish with hope, not despair |
| **Frustrations** | Games that exploit trauma for shock; Korea shown as exotic set dressing; no way to control how graphic it gets |
| **Penrose scenario** | Reads a review mentioning surgery as a graduation gift and buys it. Uses the Symbolic intensity level. The Family Room is loudest for her. She cries at the Good ending, reads the creator's note, and opens the support-resources screen |
| **What Penrose must do for her** | Specific, accurate Korean detail; respect for the subject; three intensity levels; a recovery-oriented ending; support resources; a creator's note |

## 3. Partner personas (the "B2B" side)

### C1 · Marcus Hale, horror Let's Player (creator)

> *"Give me a blind playthrough under four hours with one moment my chat will lose it over, and don't get my video demonetized."*

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Role** | Full-time YouTuber and Twitch streamer, 450k subscribers, horror and narrative games |
| **Decides** | Which games he plays on camera; whether to do a series or a single video |
| **Goals** | Views per hour of footage; strong reactions; chat participation; games his audience trusts him to pick |
| **Frustrations** | Copyrighted music triggering claims; content that gets videos age-restricted or demonetized; long slow openings |
| **Gets keys from** | Key-distribution platforms (e.g. Keymailer, Woovit), developer emails, festivals |
| **What Penrose must do for him** | Blind-playable in 2–4 h; a **streamer-safe music** option; a printable **content-warning card** for stream start; "spot what changed" moments for chat; a press kit and early keys |

### C2 · Elena Duarte, business developer at a porting publisher

> *"Show me the wishlists, the review score, and that your engine can ship on console."*

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Role** | Business development manager at a publisher that ports successful PC indies to Switch, PlayStation and Xbox |
| **Decides** | Which titles to sign and on what revenue share |
| **Goals** | A low-risk port with a proven audience; a title that fits the publisher's narrative-horror catalog |
| **Frustrations** | Engines that are hard to port; content that may fail console certification or ratings; developers without data |
| **What Penrose must do for her** | Clear launch metrics (wishlists, reviews, Twitch/YouTube); a codebase that can be ported (Godot console ports exist through third-party porting specialists); age ratings prepared early; content settings that help certification |

### C3 · Dr. Priya Nair, partnerships lead at a body-image and eating-disorder charity

> *"We can point people to a game like this, but only if it follows safe-messaging guidance and leads players to real help."*

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Role** | Communications and partnerships lead at a charity |
| **Decides** | Whether the charity reviews, endorses or links to a piece of media |
| **Goals** | Raise awareness without harm; get accurate helpline information in front of people who need it |
| **Frustrations** | Media that glamorizes thinness or procedures, shows numbers or methods, or treats disorders as a twist |
| **What Penrose must do for her** | Follow safe-messaging rules (no numbers, no methods, no glamorized "after"); accurate support resources; a sensitivity review before launch |

## 4. Assumptions to validate

Tested in the [player research kit](01-player-research/README.md) (#28).

| # | Assumption | Persona | Risk if wrong |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A1 | Narrative-horror fans want choices that change the ending, and replay for them | P1 | Endings and replay design are wasted |
| A2 | Players don't guess what a random-seeming personality quiz is measuring | P1, P2 | The quiz disguise fails |
| A3 | Dress-up and cute-horror fans would play a game about beauty standards, if it doesn't mock them | P2 | Loses a primary audience |
| A4 | A free browser slice on itch.io leads to Steam wishlists | P2 | Pre-launch funnel fails |
| A5 | Players with lived experience will play if they can control intensity | P3 | Excludes the audience the game is for |
| A6 | Korean cultural detail interests international players instead of confusing them | P1, P2, P3 | Story feels inaccessible |
| A7 | $9.99–$14.99 for 2–4 h feels fair | All | Pricing hurts conversion |
| A8 | Male players relate to the Stronger path | P1 | Male players feel the game isn't for them |
| A9 | Creators will cover a game about body image if it's monetization-safe | C1 | Main discovery channel fails |
